You know the feeling. An hour after a big lunch you cannot keep your eyes open,
and you assume you are tired or bored or the room is too warm.

Usually it is none of those. It is your blood sugar going up fast and then coming
down fast, and the crash is the part you feel.

Here is the rule, and then the reasoning, so you can decide for yourself when it
applies.

**Eat fibre and protein before the carbohydrates. Sleep properly. Move after
meals.** That is most of it.

## What is actually happening

Everything you eat that contains carbohydrate — bread, rice, pasta, fruit,
anything sweet — gets broken down into glucose and goes into your blood. Your
body then has to get it out of your blood and into your cells, and it does that
with insulin.

If the glucose arrives slowly, that is easy. If it arrives all at once, your body
overcorrects — it clears too much, you drop below where you started, and that
dip is the fog you feel at 2pm.

So the goal is not to avoid carbohydrates. It is to make them arrive slowly.

## Why the order of your food matters

This is the most useful thing on this page and it costs you nothing.

Eating the same meal in a different order changes how fast the glucose arrives.
Fibre first — vegetables, salad — then protein and fat, then the carbohydrate.
The fibre and protein slow everything behind them down. Same food, same calories,
smaller spike.

You do not have to eat differently. You have to eat in a different order.

## Why sleep is on this list

Because it belongs there, even though it sounds unrelated.

Sleep badly and your body handles glucose worse the next day — you get a bigger
spike from the same meal. It also makes you hungrier, and specifically hungrier
for fast carbohydrate, which is the thing you were trying to eat less of.

The three that actually work: do not eat late, so you are not digesting while
asleep. Get properly tired during the day rather than napping through it. And
keep caffeine to the morning — it lasts far longer in you than it feels like it
does.

## Why stress is on it too

Stress raises blood sugar directly. Your body is preparing you to run away from
something, and it does that by putting fuel in your blood. Useful once. Not
useful when the thing you are running from is a deadline you have had for three
weeks.

Which means some of this is not about food at all. Leaving things until the last
minute has a physical cost, and this is one of the places it shows up.

## The rest, briefly

**Move after eating.** A ten minute walk after a meal does more than you would
think, because working muscles pull glucose out of your blood directly.

**Drink water.** Dehydration concentrates everything in your blood, glucose
included.

**Prefer food closer to its original form.** Not a moral rule — a mechanical one.
The more something has been broken down before you eat it, the less breaking down
your body has to do, and the faster it arrives.

## What I am confident about, and what I am not

The mechanism is established: glucose rises after eating, insulin clears it, and
the size of the swing depends on what and how you ate. The food-order effect is
well studied.

What I am less certain about is how much any of it matters *for you*, at your
age, with your body. I worked this out because I needed to, and what I am
describing is what worked for me. You may find the afternoon crash barely
registers, in which case leave it.

The reason I am telling you now, while it is not a problem, is that the habits
are cheap to build at your age and expensive to build at mine. Eating salad
before rice costs nothing. Learning to do it at fifty, after someone hands you a
diagnosis, costs a great deal more.

That is the whole argument. Do what you like with it.
